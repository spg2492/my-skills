# Analysis Spec — QBR Analysis

## PathFactory Experience Context

Use this section to interpret performance across PathFactory modules and experience types. Not all modules are equivalent — some are modern platform capabilities, some are Classic Experiences (Target, Recommend, and Microsites), some support always-on website personalization, and others support revenue intelligence and seller activation workflows. This context must inform the executive summary narrative, Where You're Seeing Value, Growth Opportunities, and Recommended Actions. Do not treat all modules as interchangeable.

---

### Content Playlists

**What it is:**
Content Playlists are guided, multi-asset PathFactory experiences that let visitors browse a curated sequence of content without leaving the page. They support personalized journeys, detailed analytics, and flexible use cases such as nurture programs, ABM content hubs, thematic journeys, and educational sequences.

**Strong performance indicates:**
Strong Playlist performance signals that the content journey is structured well enough to keep visitors consuming multiple assets in one session. High engagement, binge behavior, and downstream conversion in this format usually mean the customer is not just attracting attention, but guiding buyers deeper into a cohesive journey.

**Low performance may indicate:**
Low Playlist performance often points to a sequencing, relevance, or configuration issue rather than simply weak content demand. If visitors are not progressing through multiple assets, the likely issues are that the journey is not aligned to audience intent, the content mix is too broad or too shallow, or personalization is not helping visitors find the right next step. This should be interpreted as an optimization opportunity rather than proof that the audience is not interested.

**Guidance:**
When Content Playlists perform well, frame them as a sign of content journey maturity and recommend scaling the same journey structure across additional campaigns, segments, or accounts. When they underperform, recommend reviewing content sequencing, asset relevance, content tagging, and personalization setup before making broader conclusions about content quality.

---

### Templated Experiences

**What it is:**
Templated Experiences are PathFactory's reusable, scalable digital experience framework. They support customizable landing-page and microsite-style experiences for campaign hubs, ABM experiences, partner resource centers, event marketing, and other repeatable motions. They are designed to help teams launch and replicate structured experiences more efficiently. They can also support Dynamic Personalization using Segments and Segment Rules to tailor copy, logos, images, and other experience elements for different audiences without requiring separate experiences to be built from scratch.

**Strong performance indicates:**
Strong performance in Templated Experiences usually signals that targeting, structure, and conversion design are working together effectively. Because these experiences are often built for specific campaigns, accounts, or segments, high engagement or form performance can reflect strong audience fit and a scalable campaign design that can be reused in other motions. High form rate at lower visitor volume can still be a strong signal if the audience is intentionally narrow and high intent. Where Dynamic Personalization is in use, strong performance can also indicate that audience-specific tailoring is improving relevance and helping different segments engage more effectively.

**Low performance may indicate:**
Low performance may mean the experience is not sufficiently tailored to its intended audience, the page is not fully optimized for conversion, or the traffic source is not well matched to the experience design. Since Templated Experiences are meant to be configurable and reusable, underperformance should generally be interpreted first as a targeting, personalization, content, or conversion design issue rather than as proof the format itself is ineffective. Where Dynamic Personalization is in use, weak performance may also point to poor segment logic, overly broad audience rules, weak fallback content, or personalization that is not meaningfully improving relevance.

**Guidance:**
When Templated Experiences perform well, call them out as a scalability asset and recommend replicating the strongest patterns across more campaigns, geographies, accounts, or use cases. When they underperform, recommend reviewing personalization setup, page configuration, content quality, traffic-source alignment, and conversion strategy using the same launch-readiness and optimization lens. Where Dynamic Personalization is part of the setup, recommendations should also consider reviewing segment definitions, rule logic, fallback content, and whether the personalized elements are actually improving the buyer journey.

---

### Website Tools

**What it is:**
Website Tools is PathFactory's on-site content discovery, recommendation, and personalization motion that extends PathFactory beyond standalone campaign destinations into the customer's website experience. It should be interpreted as an always-on engagement layer that helps visitors discover relevant next content while they are already on the website, rather than requiring every content journey to begin on a dedicated PathFactory destination.

**Strong performance indicates:**
Strong Website Tools performance signals that recommendation placement, content relevance, and the available content pool are helping visitors discover and consume additional content directly on the customer's website. Treat this as evidence that the customer is building a stronger self-serve buyer journey on-site, not just within campaign experiences. It is a sign of always-on discovery maturity and stronger content activation beyond one-time campaign traffic.

**Low performance may indicate:**
Website Tools may be underperforming because recommendation placements are not prominent, the content source or sitemap coverage is incomplete, the content library is not rich enough to support strong recommendations, or the recommendations are not matching visitor intent well. Low performance should be interpreted first as a content discovery and configuration issue, not automatically as a lack of buyer interest.

**Guidance:**
When Website Tools perform well, highlight that the customer is creating a more scalable, always-on content discovery motion and recommend expanding successful placements or improving coverage across more pages. When Website Tools underperform, recommend reviewing tracking setup, content source coverage, recommendation placement strategy, and the breadth and quality of available content before scaling further.

---

### PathFactory for Revenue Intelligence (PFRI)

**What it is:**
PathFactory for Revenue Intelligence (PFRI) provides revenue intelligence dashboards and buyer, account, and opportunity insights for marketers and sellers. It also supports seller workflows, including personalized content sharing and AI-generated content recommendations available from buyer-level workflows. PFRI should be interpreted as a revenue intelligence and seller-activation motion, not as a generic recommendation engine.

**Strong performance indicates:**
Strong PFRI usage or strong buyer-level engagement signals that the customer is not just measuring content engagement, but turning first-party buying signals into seller action. High-value signals here include engaged buyers, active accounts or opportunities, and effective seller follow-up using shared PathFactory Experiences or recommended content. Treat this as evidence of stronger marketing-to-sales alignment and more actionable use of engagement data in revenue workflows.

**Low performance may indicate:**
Low PFRI value may mean the customer is not yet operationalizing buyer and account insights in seller workflows, sellers are not actively sharing content from Salesforce or PFRI, or the underlying engagement signals are not yet translating into prioritized follow-up. In this case, the opportunity is not just to improve recommendations, but to strengthen how revenue teams use buyer insights, account signals, and curated content-sharing motions in practice.

**Guidance:**
Use PFRI to interpret revenue relevance, not just content performance. When signals are strong, recommend scaling seller enablement motions around buyer-level insights, account prioritization, and content sharing. When PFRI usage or outcomes are weak, recommend improving adoption of seller workflows, clarifying which signals should trigger follow-up, and ensuring sellers have access to relevant curated content and landing pages they can share directly with buyers.

### Target

**What it is:**
Target Tracks are a Classic Experience in PathFactory. They are collections of content selected and organized to create a specific content journey, with the marketer maintaining direct control over which content appears and in what order.

**Strong performance indicates:**
Strong Target performance shows there is still real demand for the journey or content theme being delivered. Because Target gives the marketer direct control over the sequence, good performance can indicate that the audience-content match is strong and that the manually curated path is resonating. Treat this as proof of audience demand and a working content theme, not necessarily as proof the customer is using the most scalable format.

**Low performance may indicate:**
Low Target performance may suggest the journey has become stale, the content order no longer matches audience intent, or the experience lacks the flexibility and personalization depth now available in more modern PathFactory modules. High traffic with weak engagement or conversion should be interpreted as a sign that the audience exists, but the experience design is not capturing enough value from that demand.

**Guidance:**
When Target performs well, acknowledge the demand signal but do not treat it as the end-state recommendation. Use strong Target results as evidence of a working topic, audience, or campaign motion, then recommend modernizing the strongest journeys into Content Playlists or Templated Experiences where the customer can get more scalability, stronger personalization, and more flexible optimization.

---

### Recommend

**What it is:**
Recommend is a Classic Experience in PathFactory that surfaces additional content to visitors through a recommendation-based track. It is part of the Classic Experiences family and can be used to keep visitors exploring beyond their initial content interaction.

**Strong performance indicates:**
Strong Recommend performance suggests visitors are willing to continue exploring content beyond the initial asset or entry point. In practice, this can signal that the recommendation logic, content pool, and thematic grouping are relevant enough to keep buyers moving through additional assets. Treat this as a useful signal of content themes and next-step interest, even if the module itself is not the most modern recommendation motion in the platform.

**Low performance may indicate:**
Low Recommend performance may indicate that the content pool is too narrow, the recommendations are not relevant enough, or the visitor's next step is not clear or compelling. This should usually be interpreted as a content discovery and recommendation design issue rather than a blanket statement that the audience lacks interest.

**Guidance:**
When Recommend performs well, use it to identify which themes, assets, and content types are strong candidates for broader journey design. When it underperforms, recommend reviewing content variety, tagging, and recommendation strategy, and consider whether the customer should shift high-value motions into Content Playlists or Website Tools where more modern recommendation and personalization capabilities can support a stronger next-step experience.

---

### Microsites

**What it is:**
Microsites are Classic Experiences designed to create tailored, immersive multi-page destinations. They can include multiple content tracks, navigation across pages, and custom page sections, making them suitable for educational hubs, event destinations, or campaign-specific resource centers.

**Strong performance indicates:**
Strong Microsite performance signals that the campaign, event, or audience destination is attracting the right visitors and giving them enough value to keep exploring. Because Microsites are often used as richer destinations, good engagement or conversion here suggests there is meaningful audience interest in the subject area or program being presented. Treat this as proof of campaign or audience demand, not proof that Microsites are the best long-term framework.

**Low performance may indicate:**
Low Microsite performance may mean the destination is outdated, too broad, insufficiently personalized, or no longer aligned to an active campaign need. Since Microsites sit in the Classic Experiences family, underperformance can also indicate that the customer would benefit from a more reusable and modern framework for future campaign destinations.

**Guidance:**
When Microsites are still driving meaningful engagement, acknowledge the audience demand but frame them as modernization candidates rather than the ideal long-term format. Recommend rebuilding the most valuable Microsite motions as Templated Experiences where the customer can gain more reusability, stronger governance, and a more scalable framework for campaign execution. If Microsites are inactive or weak, flag them for retirement or migration review rather than continued expansion.

### Interpretation Guidance

Apply the following lenses when generating narrative sections, strengths, opportunities, and recommendations:

- **Buyer journey depth:** Use binge rate, views per session, session time, and conversion signals to assess whether buyers are moving beyond the first touchpoint into a deeper content journey. This is especially important for guided experiences like Content Playlists and for richer destinations like Microsites or Templated Experiences.

- **Personalization maturity:** Distinguish between Classic Experiences and PathFactory's more modern, scalable modules. Target, Recommend, and Microsites can still generate demand, but Content Playlists, Templated Experiences, and Website Tools reflect a more modern personalization and journey-design approach. Within Templated Experiences, the use of Dynamic Personalization should be interpreted as an additional maturity signal when the data or context shows it is in use.

- **Revenue activation maturity:** Use PFRI signals to assess whether the customer is operationalizing buyer engagement data in seller workflows. Strong buyer, account, or opportunity signals paired with action by revenue teams should be interpreted as a more advanced revenue-enablement motion, not just strong reporting.

- **Campaign scalability:** When Templated Experiences and Content Playlists perform well, frame that as evidence the customer has reusable campaign architecture that can be extended to additional use cases. When Classic Experiences carry most of the traffic or conversion load, frame that as a dependency risk because those motions may be less reusable and less aligned to PathFactory's more current experience model.

- **Always-on discovery maturity:** Use Website Tools performance to assess whether the customer is extending PathFactory into an always-on website motion. Strong results indicate the customer is building self-serve discovery paths for visitors, not relying only on discrete campaign destinations.

- **Modernization opportunities:** Strong performance in a Classic Experience should be treated as proof of audience demand, not proof the customer should keep investing in the legacy format. Where Target, Recommend, or Microsites are working, use that performance to identify what should be migrated, scaled, or rebuilt in a more modern PathFactory module.

- **Do not treat modules as equivalent:** Similar conversion rates across different modules do not mean the same thing. A high-converting Templated Experience built for a narrow audience and a broader-performing Classic Experience can both be valuable, but they reflect different levels of targeting precision, journey design, scalability, and personalization maturity. Always interpret performance in the context of what the module is designed to do.

## Context and QBR Goal Usage

If a customer context file, quarterly notes file, or QBR goal / QBR brief file is available, use it to interpret the data more accurately, prioritize the analysis, and tailor the narrative for the current reporting period.

These context inputs may include:
- business goals and success metrics
- target audience or segments
- active campaigns or key use cases
- known challenges or risks
- PathFactory maturity and module adoption
- stakeholder priorities for the QBR period
- the purpose of the meeting (for example: renewal, expansion, adoption review, performance recovery, executive alignment)
- the intended audience
- the desired business outcome of the QBR
- the key priorities or concerns for the quarter

Use these inputs with the following priority order:
1. **QBR goal / QBR brief** — use this first to determine the purpose of the meeting, what matters most in the current quarter, and how the narrative should be prioritized
2. **Customer context / quarterly notes** — use this second to understand the customer's business priorities, maturity, use cases, risks, and stakeholder context
3. **PathFactory context** — use this third to interpret what product/module performance means and what kinds of recommendations are valid within the platform

Use customer context and QBR goal inputs to:
- determine which findings matter most
- shape the executive summary
- frame strengths in terms of business value
- prioritize growth opportunities
- prioritize recommended actions
- tailor the narrative to the purpose of the meeting and the customer's maturity

When the QBR meeting purpose is known, use it to shape the narrative emphasis:
- **Renewal** — lead with value delivered and ROI continuity. Anchor the executive summary on what the data shows is working. Frame growth opportunities as ways to protect and extend that value, not as risks.
- **Expansion** — lead with untapped potential. Highlight strong performance as proof of scalability, and use growth opportunities to make the case for broader adoption or new use cases.
- **Adoption review** — lead with activation gaps. Focus on which modules or capabilities are underutilized relative to what the customer has access to. Recommended actions should prioritize enablement and configuration.
- **Performance recovery** — lead with what is improving, then acknowledge what still needs attention. Avoid a purely critical tone. Frame every gap as an addressable problem with a specific path forward.
- **Executive alignment** — prioritize the strategic takeaway and executive summary above all else. Keep the narrative at business outcome level. Reduce emphasis on granular metrics in the narrative sections.

If the meeting purpose is not specified, default to a balanced narrative that covers value delivered, key opportunities, and forward-looking actions in roughly equal weight.

Do not use customer context, quarterly notes, or the QBR goal as a substitute for performance data.
All metric claims, rankings, and performance conclusions must still come from the parsed source files.
If context or QBR goals conflict with the underlying data, prioritize the data and note the mismatch where relevant.

## Ranking Logic

When determining top-performing assets, experiences, or content types, always rank using this priority order:

1. **Conversions first** — form fills, CTA clicks, and downloads are the strongest signal. Rank by total conversions where available.
2. **Engagement second** — if conversion data is tied or unavailable, rank by engagement score, then binge rate, then views per session.
3. **Visitor volume third** — use as a tiebreaker only. High traffic with low engagement and zero conversions is a gap signal, not a win.

Apply this logic consistently across Content Performance (section 4), Content Type Analysis (section 5), Topic Analysis (section 6), and Experience & Time Performance (section 7).

If conversion data is missing for a section, fall back to engagement, state the limitation explicitly, and do not imply conversion performance.

## Analysis Coverage

Every report must address all of the following areas using only real data from the parsed files.

---

### 1. Visitor Trends
- Total visitors for the period
- YoY delta overall and by industry
- Top industries by visitor volume
- Industries showing growth vs. decline
- Worst declining industries
- Flag high "Undefined" industry traffic as an attribution risk

---

### 2. Engagement Analysis
- Overall engagement rate and MoM trend
- Binge rate and what it signals
- Views per session and avg session time
- Engagement score distribution across assets
- Depth of content consumption signals

---

### 3. Conversion Quality
- Overall form capture rate, CTA click rate, download rate
- Compare to prior period where data allows
- Top converting experiences
- Zero-conversion experiences with high traffic — flag as gaps
- Which forms are driving conversions (by name/ID if available)
- Which experiences are driving the most conversions
- Which content assets are driving conversions
- Which content types contribute most to conversions

---

### 4. Content Performance
- Top 5 performing content assets by views
- For each: include content type and topic tag if available in the data
- For each: include views, engagement score, form fills, and downloads where available
- Identify assets that are high-traffic but low-conversion (gap signal)
- Identify assets that are low-traffic but high-engagement (underexposed signal)

---

### 5. Content Type Analysis
- Group all assets by content type (e.g. eBook, webinar, one-pager, case study, video)
- Identify which content types drive the most views
- Identify which content types drive the highest engagement scores
- Identify which content types drive the most conversions (form fills, downloads)
- Surface any content type with high volume but low conversion as a gap

---

### 6. Topic Analysis
- If topic tags exist in the data, group assets by topic
- Identify top-performing topics by engagement score
- Identify top-performing topics by conversions
- Note any topics with high traffic but low engagement or conversion
- If topic tags are absent or sparse, flag as a data gap

---

### 7. Experience & Time Performance
- Performance by module / experience type: Content Playlists, Templated Experiences, Website Tools, Target, Recommend, Microsites, and PFRI-driven motions where applicable
- Per type: visitor counts, form rates, download rates, YoY deltas where available
- Identify best and worst performing module or experience type
- Top 3 performing individual experiences ranked by combined engagement and conversion signals
- For each top experience: include experience name, type, visitor count, form rate, download rate
- Session time trends and incremental time metrics
- Where identifiable, note whether Dynamic Personalization is being used within Templated Experiences and whether it appears to influence engagement or conversion outcomes

---

### 8. Pipeline Influence
- Total form fills, CTA clicks, and downloads as revenue-relevant signals
- S1/S2 pipeline influenced where data is available
- Closed Won attribution where data is available
- Revenue per visitor trend
- YoY trajectory of pipeline influence metrics
- Buyer, account, or opportunity-level engagement signals where PFRI data is available

---

### 9. Benchmark & Comparative Analysis (Optional)

Only include this section if benchmark or industry benchmark files were found during the validation pass.

- Compare the customer's key metrics against available benchmarks: engagement rate, form capture rate, binge rate, views per session
- Identify where the customer is outperforming the benchmark and by how much
- Identify where the customer is underperforming and flag as a priority gap
- If industry-specific benchmarks are available, use those over company-wide averages
- Label all benchmark figures clearly — state the source (e.g. "company-wide average", "industry benchmark") so the reader understands the comparison basis
- Do not fabricate benchmark figures — if no benchmark file exists, omit this section entirely

Where benchmark comparisons are strong, incorporate them into Where You're Seeing Value. Where the customer underperforms, incorporate into Growth Opportunities or Recommended Actions.

## Report Structure

Follow this structure exactly. Do not add or remove sections.

### 1. KPI Bar
Display 6 key metrics at the top of the report. Use available data in this order:
1. Total Visitors
2. Engagement Rate
3. Binge Rate
4. Form Capture Rate
5. Total Form Fills
6. CTA Click Rate (fall back to Total Downloads if CTA data is unavailable)

Each metric must show a YoY or period-over-period delta where data allows.

### 2. Executive Summary
Write 3–4 sentences only. Cover:
- Overall performance trend for the period
- The biggest value driver (what's working most)
- The biggest opportunity (what to address)
- At least one specific metric to anchor the narrative

Do not summarize every metric. Do not repeat data that appears in the supporting tables. Write for a VP or CMO — confident, outcome-focused, no jargon.

### 2b. Customer Goals Scorecard

Include this section immediately after the Executive Summary if a QBR brief or customer context file was found. This section shows the customer's stated goals alongside how the current period's data reflects progress toward each one.

**How to build it:**
- Extract the customer's primary business goals and QBR priorities from the QBR brief and customer context files
- For each goal, assess status using the data from the parsed source files:
  - **On Track** — data shows clear positive signals directly tied to this goal
  - **In Progress** — partial progress visible in the data; foundational work underway but outcome not yet achieved
  - **Not Yet Started** — goal is stated but no meaningful data signal or activation exists yet
- For each goal, include: the goal name, its status, 1–2 specific data points that support the assessment, and a single recommended next step
- Limit to 4–6 goals maximum. Combine closely related goals if needed. Do not pad with goals that aren't clearly supported by either the context files or the data.
- Do not fabricate progress. If a goal has no data signal at all, mark it "Not Yet Started" and recommend what first step would generate that signal.

**If no QBR brief or customer context file was found**, omit this section entirely.

### 3. Where You're Seeing Value
Exactly 3 strengths. Each must:
- Include a specific data point or metric
- Focus on high-performing content, experiences, or conversion signals
- Explain why it matters as a business outcome
- Reference benchmark performance if benchmark data is available and relevant
- At least one must reflect content performance, content type strength, or a top-performing experience
- If Website Tools or PFRI data is available and strong, these are valid strengths — frame Website Tools performance as always-on discovery maturity, and PFRI performance as revenue activation maturity

### 4. Growth Opportunities
1–2 areas for improvement. Each must:
- Be supported by specific data evidence
- Be framed constructively — as an opportunity, not a failure
- Examples: zero-conversion high-traffic experiences, underperforming content types, missing topic coverage, attribution gaps, low Website Tools coverage, underutilized PFRI seller workflows

### 5. Recommended Actions
Exactly 3 actions. Each must be:
- Tied directly to a specific finding from the analysis
- Specific and actionable — not generic advice
- Prioritized by potential impact on engagement, conversion, or pipeline
- At least one must address content or experience optimization
- If PFRI or Website Tools data supports a finding, recommended actions in those areas are valid and should be included when the data warrants it

### 6. Strategic Takeaway
One sentence. The single most important insight a CEO could repeat in a meeting.

### 7. Supporting Data Tables
After the narrative, include all relevant data tables as defined in `html-template.md`. The tables carry the detailed data — the narrative should reference key figures but not reproduce full datasets.

### 8. Optional Sections
Include only if data exists:
- Benchmark Comparison table
- Conversion Drivers table
- Data Gaps & Notes

## Data Availability Rules

These rules govern how to handle missing, sparse, or ambiguous data. They exist to prevent fabricated insights.

- **Never estimate or infer a metric that is not present in the data.** If a field is blank, zero, or absent, treat it as unavailable — not as zero performance.
- **If a core analysis section cannot be completed** due to a missing file, state clearly in the report: what data was missing, which section was affected, and what could not be assessed as a result.
- **If a metric exists but is sparse** (e.g. topic tags on fewer than 20% of assets), complete the analysis on what is available and flag the coverage gap explicitly.
- **If conversion data is missing at the asset or form level**, fall back to experience-level conversion data. If that is also missing, fall back to aggregate totals. State the fallback level used.
- **Do not combine metrics across incompatible time periods** without noting the mismatch. If one file covers Q1 and another covers full-year, label each figure with its period.
- **Benchmark figures must come from a parsed benchmark file.** Do not use recalled or assumed industry averages.
- **If two files contain conflicting values for the same metric**, flag the conflict, use the more granular or recent source, and note which source was used.
- **If module-specific data is not available** for Website Tools, PFRI, or Dynamic Personalization within Templated Experiences, do not force a module-specific narrative. Only reference those modules or capabilities when the underlying files or context support them.

---

## Writing Rules

- Write for a VP or CMO, not a data analyst
- Every number must be interpreted in context — never list raw data
- No jargon — if you use a platform term, briefly explain it
- No vague recommendations — every next step must be traceable to a specific finding
- Connect every metric to a business outcome
- If data is missing for a section, note the gap and explain what it means — do not skip or fabricate
- No single-metric storytelling — always connect multiple signals to tell a complete story
- Tone and emphasis must reflect the purpose of the QBR meeting — see Context and QBR Goal Usage for how each meeting type should shape the narrative
